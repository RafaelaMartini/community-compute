# Community Compute: sharing idle computing power for access to AI

Hi! I'm Rafaela, an artist, and I'd like to discuss an idea with people who work with local AI and distributed computing.

The inspiration comes from sharing networks such as BitTorrent and projects such as BOINC: could we use available computing power on people's computers to provide AI to the community itself, without a mandatory monthly subscription?

Each participant would choose when and how much to contribute, providing computing power and the electricity needed to run AI tasks. In return, they would receive access proportional to the useful work they helped perform. The idea is to exchange resources for access, recognizing that electricity and hardware use have a cost.

The "brain" would be a pretrained model whose license permits this use. A coordinating program would route requests to available devices. For a first experiment, I imagine distributing complete tasks across computers.

I know distributed computing and collaborative inference already exist. I'd like to understand what we can build on from existing projects and whether this contribution-and-access mechanism makes practical sense.

A small first test could involve:

- two computers running a text model;
- a queue that routes tasks to an available machine;
- controls to participate, pause, and set schedules;
- an experimental record of contributions and usage;
- performance measurements and a cost assessment.

Initially, we would use only test requests containing no personal information. Privacy on other people's machines, security, work validation, fraud, energy consumption, and balancing contributions against usage are open questions. There is no promise of unlimited access, and economic viability has not been demonstrated.

At this stage, this is an exploratory proposal, with no prototype or funding. I have a Mac that could be used for testing, but I have no technical background or availability to coordinate development every day. I'm looking for people interested in evaluating the idea and, especially, someone interested in leading the technical side of a possible experiment. Suggestions of existing projects it would make more sense to contribute to are also welcome.

This is an independent initiative with no affiliation with AI companies. This text was organized with AI assistance; discussion follow-up may also be handled by an assistant on my behalf, identified as such.

**What is the smallest experiment that could show whether exchanging computing power for AI access can work?**
